INTELLIGENCE

The Intelligence Operating System

"Intelligence scores it. Markets sell it. The receipt proves it. Readiness before activation. Evidence before capital."

Intelligence Ecosystem owns and operates the Corridor Intelligence Platform, licensing it to specialist divisions — Intelligence Diagnostics, Intelligence Programs, and TWNS Apex — while Global Markets and Media Network consume its output under license. Intelligence itself never contracts a customer directly: it scores, sources, and publishes corridor-readiness intelligence so that every capital decision rests on evidence, not assumption.

Platform Architecture

Intelligence Platform (CIP)

The shared runtime Intelligence Inc. runs across its divisions: it ingests signals, classifies every record against the nine Frontiers, processes the data through the five unit lenses, scores corridors and destinations, and publishes the output to the license API and the Master Intelligence Desk.

Capital Index-SCI

The pricing layer. A quarterly composite that prices destination readiness for tourism- anchored sovereign capital across four pillars — Demand, Connectivity, Macro, and Capital. 199 jurisdictions, five bands, one yardstick.

Global Index-GCI

Scores ordered origin-to-destination corridor pairs across six sub-scores — corridor readiness, aviation viability, convening density, diaspora velocity, investment flow, and readiness gap — recalculating continuously as cleared signals land.

Frontiers

The nine Frontiers are IP's master taxonomy; every ingested signal, score component, and readiness output carries one or more Frontier tags, making it possible for an aviation datapoint and a diaspora datapoint to sit in the same argument.

Pulse

The Pulse Vitality Engine is the free public front door that converts a ten-question destination self-report into a vitality score and valuation band, writing the submission into IP as a first-class signal.

Pipeline

The process layer that carries a corridor from ingested signal classified record scored profile activation candidate, with SCI on the Intelligence-to-Capital seam and CDU on the Capital-to-Activation seam.

Intelligence Diagnostics

Intelligence Diagnostics — The CRA Engine

Intelligence Diagnostics (IDU) is the sole contracting party for the Corridor Readiness Assessment. It issues the dated, conditioned verdict, signs the deliverable, holds the methodology version, and delivers remediation against the conditions named in the verdict.

A CRA answers one question: "Should this corridor carry a mandate?" It is not an index — it is a binding institutional opinion.

!

A CRA is only citable once ratified by the Methodology Council; until then, it ships as an Unratified Working Reading.

Intelligence Programs — Five Desks. Five Recurring Decisions.

Intelligence Programs (IS) operates five unit desks delivering recurring IP-sourced intelligence products. It sells no verdicts — only licensed access to dated, corridor-specific reads.

MIRCU — Marketing Intelligence, Research & Corridor Unit

Produces a dated weekly demand read from search intent, rate movement, and occupancy data for retained corridors.

BIMAU — Business Intelligence & Market Access Unit

Maps visa regimes, permits, and bilateral rules into an executable route through market-entry bureaucracy.

AIRCU — Aviation Intelligence, Research & Corridor Unit

Tests proposed routes against capacity, traffic rights, and economics, returning a conditioned flyability verdict.

DIRCU — Destination Intelligence, Research & Corridor Unit

Maintains a continuously scored readiness position for each destination against peer-band standing and the nine Frontiers.

EINDU — Economic Intelligence, Networking & Development Unit

Aggregates all four desk outputs into a bankability and risk assessment written in the language capital underwrites.

The Global Corridor Intelligence Brief

One page. Read it in ninety seconds.

What It Is

The weekly intelligence record of the world's tourism, aviation, and capital corridors — delivered every Friday as a forty-five-minute video brief and written note. It is the flagship publication of NSTEDPath Intelligence, and the only place where the corridor desks go on record about what a corridor is worth doing. Not a newsletter of announcements. A judgment you can be held to.

Why It Exists

Corridor decisions — routes, resorts, sovereign guarantees — are still made on brochures and optimism. The Brief replaces that with a dated, sourced, conditioned position for every corridor it covers.

What's Inside Every Edition

  • • Corridor of the week — Demand, access, capital, capacity, and readiness, opened up in full
  • • Desk filings — Live evidence from MIRCU on the visitor economy and AIRCU on access and aviation
  • • The verdict — Viable · Conditional · Not Viable — dated, with conditions named
  • • Field note — Recorded on the floor of the fair or convening attended that week
  • • Coverage disclosure — What's missing, and what that does to confidence
Three rules, printed on every edition.

How the Brief Earns Trust

01

Synthesize, Don't Copy

Every number restated in our units, our frame, our words. No screenshots, ever.

02

Good-Faith Citation

Source, date, and access date wherever the license permits. If we can't cite it properly, we don't use it.

03

Name What We Don't Have

Below the coverage floor, a corridor is published as unobserved — never as zero, never with a verdict it hasn't earned.

Who writes it: The author is human. Desk leads draft from their own filings; the Methodology Council clears every verdict, band, and condition before the script is cut; the producer publishes and owns corrections. Editions without verdict language ship as commentary, and say so.

Who it's for: Ministries and destination authorities sizing a corridor. DFIs and investors underwriting one. Airlines, hotel groups, and operators deciding where to place capacity.
